Understanding "What You Stand For"

What Is This Quiz?

What You Stand For is a comprehensive political spectrum quiz that goes beyond simple left-right classifications. Instead of placing you on a single axis, it identifies the specific policies you support across four major domains of political life, creating a detailed ideological profile that reflects the complexity of real political beliefs.

Key Insight: Most political quizzes reduce your beliefs to a single point on a line. This quiz recognizes that political beliefs are multi-dimensional. You might support free markets but also strong environmental regulations, or favor democratic governance while supporting traditional social values. This quiz captures that complexity.

Question Types

The quiz uses several question types to capture the nuance of political beliefs:

  • Single-select: Choose one option from several mutually exclusive choices
  • Multi-select: Choose all options that apply to your beliefs
  • Double questions: Two related questions presented together, where the answer to one may affect the other

Answering Questions

For each question, you can:

  • Select an answer: Choose the option(s) that best reflect your beliefs
  • Abstain: Skip the question if you're unsure or have no strong opinion
  • Partial abstention: For double questions, you can abstain from individual parts

Understanding Your Results

Your Policy Profile

Your results show all the policies you selected, organized by category and subcategory. Each policy includes a brief description explaining what it means. You can click on any policy to expand its description.

Historical Figure Matching

One of the unique features of this quiz is the ability to compare your policy positions with historical figures. The quiz currently includes profiles of 10 figures spanning different eras and ideologies:

  • John Locke (1689–1704): Classical liberalism and natural rights
  • Thomas Paine (1791–1797): Radical republicanism and proto-welfare
  • Maximilien Robespierre (1789–1794): Jacobin republic of virtue
  • Edmund Burke (1770–1797): Whig constitutionalism and ordered liberty
  • John Stuart Mill (1865–1873): Liberal socialism and utilitarian radicalism
  • Alexis de Tocqueville (1835–1851): Liberal constitutionalism and democratic analysis
  • Mohandas K. Gandhi (1931–1948): Nonviolent swaraj and trusteeship
  • Ludwig von Mises (1927–1966): Classical liberalism and Austrian economics
  • Adolf Hitler (1933–1945): National Socialist Germany
  • Barack Obama (2009–2017): Modern American liberalism
Important Note: Historical figure matching is for educational purposes only. A high similarity score does not mean you share all beliefs with that figure, nor does it imply endorsement of their actions. The matching algorithm compares policy positions, not moral character or historical context.

How Matching Works

The matching algorithm uses a bidirectional agreement model:

  • Agreement: Both you and the figure selected the same policy, or both opposed it
  • Disagreement: You selected a policy the figure opposed, or vice versa
  • Null: The figure's position is unknown or the question is anachronistic

The similarity percentage represents the proportion of comparable policies where you agree. You can adjust how many historical figures to display in Settings.

Limitations and Considerations

Quiz Limitations

  • Anachronistic questions: Some questions ask about concepts that didn't exist in certain historical periods
  • Missing nuances: Some historical positions cannot be fully represented by the available answer options
  • Modern framing: Questions are framed for contemporary political contexts
  • Cultural bias: Questions may reflect Western political traditions more than others

Historical Figure Limitations

  • Profile accuracy: Historical profiles are approximations — mapping complex thinkers onto 621 fixed policy options inevitably distorts their views
  • Null values: Many policies are marked as null for historical figures due to anachronism or lack of documentation
  • Historical context: Matching doesn't account for historical context or evolution of beliefs
  • Scholarly debate: Historical interpretations may be contested among scholars

Privacy and Data

All data is stored locally in your browser. No data is sent to any server. Your quiz results, saved profiles, and settings remain on your device.

  • Local storage: All data is stored in your browser's local storage
  • No tracking: No analytics or tracking is used
  • No accounts: No user accounts or registration required
  • Export/import: You can export and import your profiles for backup
